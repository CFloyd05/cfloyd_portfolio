## Who is Corvus Energy

Corvus Energy is the world's leading supplier of battery energy storage systems for the maritime industry; powering electric and hybrid marine vessels.


## My Projects

### Tests

#### Certification Test Journals

In addition to documenting and assisting with the tests, I collected calibration data. 

#### Thermal Build

Built a battery module with ~40 internal thermocouples for a temperature characterization test. Involved extensive communication and collaboration with the production team to modify existing processes

#### Capacitance and Resistance Characterization

Using an LCR meter and a four-lead micro-ohm meter. Developped a model for system capacitance based on configuration details. Measured power-path resistance, contactor resistance and fuse resistance.

#### Safety Fault Rection Time

Used a sensor breakout board to simulate a cell over-temperature event. Setup data-aquisition to the temperature sensor and customer-side battery terminals to measure reaction time.


### Python Data-Analysis

#### Thermal Cycle Fatigue Calculator

I wrote a thermal cycle fatigue calculator script, which will calculate a relative damage value for each customer system. The damage value can be compared to accelerated lifcycle tests, to predict system failures and validate reliability estimates. 

#### System Availability Analyzer

Using data from customer vessels, I wrote a script to calculate system availability. Availability is defined by mean uptime (MUT) divided by mean time between failures (MTBF). 

### Test-Setups

#### Insulated Enclosure

Build an enclosure from aluminum extrusions and insulating foam to decrease the thermal cycle time for an accelerated lifecycle test. I installed ducting leading to an air chiller, with a relay tapped into the system fans. The enclosure reduced thermal cycling time from 6 hours to 4, this will greatly increase the effectiveness of the accelerated lifecycle test over the coming months and years.

#### Acoustic Barriers

I built five 4"x8" acoustic barriers to block sound coming from some high-power electronics. The frames were built from 1x4s, the triangular bases were bui;lt from 2x4s, and there was a acoutic insulation panel overtop
